# Backend and numerical references

Use the recovery PyTorch hierarchical NeRF for dense vanilla, eight-view vanilla and eight-view regularised. The previous Kaggle log records 28.926 dB on dense Lego with the project's PyTorch pipeline. Its sparse run failed. Keeping the same camera and image pipeline lets us inspect that failure and instrument density and gradients without porting the whole experiment. Recovery uses positive softplus density and finite integration intervals for **every** arm. A fresh dense run with this version must establish its own score before sparse comparisons. ## Nerfstudio vanilla-nerf

In v1.1.5 this preset selects the Blender parser, two NeRF fields and RAdam at 0.0005 with no scheduler. It uses 64 uniform samples and 128 importance samples, separate coarse and fine RGB losses, white background, and default near/far 2 and 6. Its Blender parser reads the selected split's transforms file and defaults to white alpha compositing. These are framework reference settings. [Method config](https://github.com/nerfstudio-project/nerfstudio/blob/v1.1.5/nerfstudio/configs/method_configs.py#L340-L357) · [Vanilla model](https://github.com/nerfstudio-project/nerfstudio/blob/v1.1.5/nerfstudio/models/vanilla_nerf.py) · [Bounds and losses](https://github.com/nerfstudio-project/nerfstudio/blob/v1.1.5/nerfstudio/models/base_model.py#L35-L47) · [Blender parser](https://github.com/nerfstudio-project/nerfstudio/blob/v1.1.5/nerfstudio/data/dataparsers/blender_dataparser.py)

Nerfstudio lists 34.46 dB for its dense Lego implementation and 32.54 dB for the original TensorFlow NeRF. The proposal's ±2 dB window around the latter is 30.54–34.54. The recovery metric uses 25 published sparse-benchmark test views at 400 × 400. Interpret that window as a sanity check across protocols. [Nerfstudio benchmark](https://docs.nerf.studio/nerfology/methods/nerf.html#benchmarks)

## FreeNeRF Blender

We inspected the authors' **DietNeRF-pytorch/dietnerf** source at commit `c2d8bdc5b5cfa9287d1141088eb8c37b6a91eed0`. The Blender driver opens all three published train, validation and test manifests before selecting views. A direct upstream execution needs the original validation files. Recovery loads its frozen train and test inputs directly, so both local scenes are usable. [Loader](https://github.com/Jiawei-Yang/FreeNeRF/blob/c2d8bdc5b5cfa9287d1141088eb8c37b6a91eed0/DietNeRF-pytorch/dietnerf/load_blender.py#L45-L99)

The released 50k half-resolution Blender config uses 8 views, seed 0 by default, 1,024 rays per update from one image, 64 coarse + 128 importance samples, white background, the central half of the image for the first 500 updates, and near/far 2 and 6. Its fixed training selection comes from legacy NumPy choice over the 100 ordered training frames. It produces **26, 86, 2, 55, 75, 93, 16, 73**. The default test stride of eight produces **0, 8, …, 192**, or 25 held-out frames. The paper supplement lists an extra ninth ID despite describing eight views. The recovery protocol follows the executable eight-view selection and keeps initialization seeds independent. [Reference config](https://github.com/Jiawei-Yang/FreeNeRF/blob/c2d8bdc5b5cfa9287d1141088eb8c37b6a91eed0/DietNeRF-pytorch/dietnerf/configs/freenerf_8v/freenerf_8v_50k_base05.txt) · [Seed, bounds and selection](https://github.com/Jiawei-Yang/FreeNeRF/blob/c2d8bdc5b5cfa9287d1141088eb8c37b6a91eed0/DietNeRF-pytorch/dietnerf/run_nerf.py#L749-L788) · [Supplement, §B.1](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2023/supplemental/Yang_FreeNeRF_Improving_Few-Shot_CVPR_2023_supplemental.pdf)

Both positional and directional Fourier encodings reveal successive xyz triplets until the midpoint of training in the 50k sample config. Raw coordinates start visible. The occlusion term multiplies activated **fine density** at the first 20 sorted samples by 0.01 and averages the masked tensor over all rays and all sample slots, including the zeros outside those first 20. Taking the mean over the selected entries alone would over-weight this penalty. [Mask](https://github.com/Jiawei-Yang/FreeNeRF/blob/c2d8bdc5b5cfa9287d1141088eb8c37b6a91eed0/DietNeRF-pytorch/dietnerf/utils.py) · [Encoding](https://github.com/Jiawei-Yang/FreeNeRF/blob/c2d8bdc5b5cfa9287d1141088eb8c37b6a91eed0/DietNeRF-pytorch/dietnerf/run_nerf.py#L40-L59) · [Training loss](https://github.com/Jiawei-Yang/FreeNeRF/blob/c2d8bdc5b5cfa9287d1141088eb8c37b6a91eed0/DietNeRF-pytorch/dietnerf/run_nerf.py#L1064-L1085)

FreeNeRF Table 1 reports averages across **eight** Blender scenes. Its reproduced vanilla has PSNR 13.931 dB, SSIM 0.693 and LPIPS 0.320. Its regularised model has 24.259 dB, 0.883 and 0.098. The released metric helper uses VGG LPIPS. Recovery uses calibrated AlexNet v0.1 with sRGB mapped to [−1, 1], so compare its LPIPS within the recovery runs. Other sources of numerical differences include camera pixel centres, target downsampling, ray batches, integration, optimizer settings and training budget.
